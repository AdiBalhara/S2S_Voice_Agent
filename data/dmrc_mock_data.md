## [001] About DMRC
- **id:** dmrc-001
- **category:** organisation
- **tags:** DMRC, overview, history

The Delhi Metro Rail Corporation (DMRC) is the company that builds and runs the Delhi Metro. It is a joint venture of the Government of India and the Government of the National Capital Territory of Delhi. DMRC was set up in 1995. The first stretch, between Shahdara and Tis Hazari, was opened on 24 December 2002. The Delhi Metro serves Delhi and nearby NCR cities including Gurugram, Noida, Faridabad and Ghaziabad.

## [002] Why Delhi Metro is called world-class
- **id:** dmrc-002
- **category:** organisation
- **tags:** safety, technology, comfort

The official site describes the Delhi Metro as a world-class system. It uses modern communication and train-control systems for reliable and safe operation, air-conditioned coaches, and an Automatic Fare Collection (AFC) system. Entry and exit at stations is controlled by flap-doors that read smart cards and contactless tokens. The network mixes elevated, at-grade and underground sections and uses both broad-gauge and standard-gauge rolling stock.

## [003] Network size (approximate)
- **id:** dmrc-003
- **category:** network
- **tags:** network length, stations, lines
- **caution:** Sources disagree. Treat all numbers as approximate.

The Delhi Metro is the largest metro network in India. Sources published in 2026 give a core network of roughly 350 to 375 km with 10 colour-coded lines. Counts including the Aqua Line (Noida) and Rapid Metro (Gurugram) are higher, at around 400 km and 300 stations. Some aggregator sites report different totals such as 12 lines and 288 stations because they count separate operators or sections differently. One station list reports 271 stations, of which 193 are elevated, 71 underground and 7 at-grade, with 27 transfer stations.

## [004] March 2026 expansion and the ring line
- **id:** dmrc-004
- **category:** network
- **tags:** Phase 4, Pink Line, Magenta Line, Majlis Park

On 8 March 2026, the Prime Minister inaugurated two new corridors of the Delhi Metro, one on the Pink Line (about 12.3 km) and one on the Magenta Line (about 9.9 km), together adding about 22 km and 15 stations. This completed the Pink Line as India's first fully operational ring metro. Majlis Park serves as the main interchange between the two extended lines. New stations opened that day include Bhajanpura, Burari, Bhalaswa, Deepali Chowk and Haiderpur Village. The Deepali Chowk to Majlis Park Magenta Line stretch reaches a maximum elevated height of about 28.36 m.

## [005] Phase 4 and future expansion
- **id:** dmrc-005
- **category:** network
- **tags:** Phase 4, Phase 5A, expansion

Phase 4 of the Delhi Metro covers about 104 to 112 km across six corridors and is being completed in stages through 2026-28. Plans after that include a Central Vista underground corridor (RK Ashram Marg to Indraprastha), an Aerocity to IGI Airport Terminal 1 connector, and Phase 5A, which would take the total network to around 400 km. Dates are targets and may change.

## [007] Red Line
- **id:** dmrc-007
- **category:** lines
- **tags:** Red Line, Kashmere Gate

The Red Line was the first operational corridor of the Delhi Metro. It is about 34.55 km long with 29 stations and links West Delhi to East Delhi and Ghaziabad in Uttar Pradesh. Kashmere Gate on this line is one of the largest interchanges in the network.

---

## [008] Yellow Line
- **id:** dmrc-008
- **category:** lines
- **tags:** Yellow Line, Rajiv Chowk, HUDA City Centre

The Yellow Line runs north to south, from Samaypur Badli to Millennium City Centre Gurugram (earlier called HUDA City Centre). Key stations include Chandni Chowk, New Delhi, Rajiv Chowk, Central Secretariat, INA, Hauz Khas, Saket, Qutab Minar and Sikanderpur. One guide ranks it as the line with the most stations. Hauz Khas is noted as the deepest metro station in Delhi.

---

## [009] Blue Line
- **id:** dmrc-009
- **category:** lines
- **tags:** Blue Line, Noida, Dwarka

The Blue Line is the longest corridor in the network, at roughly 64.6 km with about 59 stations. It links Dwarka in West Delhi with Noida in Uttar Pradesh through central Delhi, including Rajiv Chowk, and has a branch to Vaishali.

---

## [010] Magenta Line and driverless trains
- **id:** dmrc-010
- **category:** lines
- **tags:** Magenta Line, driverless, CBTC

The Magenta Line is where India's first driverless (unmanned) metro operation began, in 2018. The Pink Line also runs driverless trains. Newer corridors use Communication-Based Train Control (CBTC), which allows very short gaps between trains (down to about 90 seconds in capability).

---

## [011] Airport Express Line
- **id:** dmrc-011
- **category:** lines
- **tags:** Airport Express, Orange Line, IGI, T3

The Airport Express (Orange Line) runs from New Delhi station to Yashobhoomi Dwarka Sector 25 via Shivaji Stadium, Dhaula Kuan, Delhi Aerocity, IGI Airport (Terminal 3) and Dwarka Sector 21. It has its own fare structure that is separate from the standard DMRC fare slabs. One guide lists the first train at about 04:45 and the last at about 23:15. Dwarka Sector 21 is the interchange with the Blue Line. Tourist cards are not valid on this line.

## [012] Airport Express fare examples
- **id:** dmrc-012
- **category:** fares
- **tags:** Airport Express, fare, combined journey
- **caution:** Third-party figures. Confirm in the DMRC app.

Journeys that combine the Airport Express with other lines are charged as two parts, an Airport Express fare plus a regular DMRC fare. One transit site lists these examples: IGI Airport to Dwarka Sector 12 costs about Rs 53 (Rs 21 + Rs 32), and Yashobhoomi Dwarka Sector 25 to Millennium City Centre costs about Rs 129 (Rs 75 + Rs 54).

## [014] Smart card and off-peak discounts
- **id:** dmrc-014
- **category:** fares
- **tags:** smart card, NCMC, discount, off-peak

Passengers using a DMRC smart card or an NCMC card get a 10 percent discount on every journey. An extra 10 percent off-peak discount applies when exiting during off-peak hours on weekdays. Off-peak hours are given as start of service until 08:00, 12:00 to 17:00, and 21:00 until end of service. On Sundays and national holidays the off-peak discount applies all day.

## [015] Ticket options
- **id:** dmrc-015
- **category:** ticketing
- **tags:** token, QR ticket, smart card, NCMC, MJQRT

Passengers can pay with a contactless token, a single-journey QR ticket, a DMRC smart card, an NCMC card, or a Multiple Journey QR Ticket (MJQRT). MJQRT launched on 13 September 2024 and works like a digital smart card inside the "DMRC Momentum Delhi Sarthi 2.0" app. It can be recharged with up to Rs 3,000 as needed. Tokens suit occasional riders, and cards suit regular commuters.

## [016] Smart (travel) card rules
- **id:** dmrc-016
- **category:** ticketing
- **tags:** smart card, recharge, validity, refund
- **caution:** Older Delhi Tourism text. Details such as deposit and limits may have changed.

Delhi Tourism describes the travel card as a stored-value card for frequent commuters. The minimum recharge it lists is Rs 100 and the maximum Rs 1,000, with later recharges in multiples of Rs 50. The card is valid for one year from purchase or last recharge, whichever is later, and renewal carries no extra charge. Balance can be checked at ticket reading machines at stations. Cards can be recharged at counters, online, or in the DMRC app.

## [017] Tourist cards
- **id:** dmrc-017
- **category:** ticketing
- **tags:** tourist card, 1 day, 3 day

Tourist cards give unlimited travel for a short period. A transit site lists the 1-day card at Rs 150 (Rs 100 fare + Rs 50 refundable deposit) and the 3-day card at Rs 500 (Rs 450 fare + Rs 50 refundable deposit). The deposit is refunded when the card is returned. Tourist cards are not valid on the Airport Express Line. Older Delhi Tourism pages list lower prices (Rs 100 and Rs 250), so confirm the current price.

## [019] Operating hours and frequency
- **id:** dmrc-019
- **category:** operations
- **tags:** timings, frequency, first train, last train

The metro generally runs from about 05:00 to midnight, with exact first and last train times varying by line and station. During peak hours trains come every 2 to 3 minutes on busy lines. The official site describes service as frequent, around three minutes. Always check the DMRC app for timings of a particular station.

## [020] Women's coach and penalties
- **id:** dmrc-020
- **category:** rules
- **tags:** women, safety, penalty, helpline

The first coach of every train, in the direction of running, is reserved for women, and platform signs show where it stops. Every coach also has reserved seats for women. A male passenger found in the women's coach can be fined Rs 250 under the Delhi Metro Act. If someone refuses to pay or ignores instructions they can be removed from the train and handed to the Delhi Metro Rail Police. DMRC runs special drives with flying squads. Incidents can be reported on the 24x7 helpline 155370. DMRC reported penalising more than 2,300 men in FY 2024-25.

## [021] Prohibited items and alcohol rules
- **id:** dmrc-021
- **category:** rules
- **tags:** prohibited items, luggage, alcohol, security

Dangerous items are banned. These include explosives, acids and other corrosive chemicals, petroleum products, radioactive material, inflammable solids and wet batteries. Oversized or non-personal luggage that blocks movement or evacuation is also restricted. A DMRC circular allows up to two sealed alcohol bottles per passenger, but drinking alcohol anywhere on metro premises is prohibited. One cigarette packet and one lighter or matchbox per passenger is allowed. Eating inside stations and coaches is not allowed even though food may be carried. Prohibited items can be confiscated and entry refused, and some offences carry fines or prosecution. Passengers should report unattended or suspicious bags immediately.

## [026] Mock FAQ: How do I get from the airport to the city centre?
- **id:** dmrc-faq-001
- **category:** faq (mock)
- **tags:** airport, route

**Q:** How do I reach central Delhi from IGI Airport by metro?
**A:** Take the Airport Express (Orange Line) from the IGI Airport station towards New Delhi. From New Delhi station you can change to the Yellow Line for Rajiv Chowk and onward travel. Buy a ticket or tap a card before entering.

## [027] Mock FAQ: Token or smart card?
- **id:** dmrc-faq-002
- **category:** faq (mock)
- **tags:** token, smart card, savings

**Q:** Is a smart card better than a token?
**A:** For regular travel, yes. Card users get 10 percent off every trip, plus an extra 10 percent off-peak discount on weekdays, and avoid queuing for tokens. Tokens suit one-off visitors who ride rarely.

- **category:** faq (mock)
- **tags:** luggage, size, security

**Q:** Can I carry a suitcase on the metro?
